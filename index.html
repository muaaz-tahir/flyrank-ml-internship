Title

Which pages get fixed first, when you can't fix them all?

A hand-written rule vs. a trained model, tested honestly on a calendar quarter neither had seen.

Track Machine Learning Data window Jan – Jun 2026 Author Muaaz Tahir Repo flyrank-ml-internship
Reviewer's noteEvery claim in this paper is checked for leakage before it's reported. Flags like this mark where a result needed a second look.
§0

Abstract

Content teams can't review every page every month, so something has to decide what gets looked at first. This paper starts from a simple hand-written rule built in Week 4 and asks whether a trained model can rank pages better. Using FlyRank's search performance warehouse, pages from Q1 2026 were used to train a logistic regression model, and Q2 2026, a fully separate quarter, was used to test it. The rule scored a near-perfect precision at first glance, but a closer look showed why: its main condition almost restates the label it was being scored against, which is disclosed here rather than reported as a win. On the subset where that overlap doesn't apply, the trained model shows a genuine, independent AUC of 0.782. The result is a working, ranked action queue, and an honest account of where a model earns its keep and where it doesn't.

§1

Introduction

FlyRank manages content for many clients, each with thousands of pages. Every review cycle, someone has to decide which pages are worth a content team's limited time: which ones to refresh, which to leave alone, which are quietly losing ground. Guessing wastes effort. A fixed rule like "refresh anything old and once-popular" turns out to catch almost nothing, a Week 1 finding on this same dataset showed it flags just 0.1% of pages that actually need attention.

This paper asks a narrower, testable version of that problem: given a page's search position, click-through rate, and recent trend, can a model rank it more usefully than a hand-written rule can? The decision this supports is simple and recurring, which pages a content team opens first, every cycle, when they can't open all of them.

§2

Data

All data comes from the FlyRank internship-warehouse release on Hugging Face, table fact_content_daily_performance, monthly parquet partitions spanning January 2025 through June 2026. This study uses two quarters:

451,841train page-months (Q1 2026)
641,306test page-months (Q2 2026)
25.1Mraw train rows before filtering
33.8Mraw test rows before filtering

Rows where gsc_data_available = false were dropped in both periods, these are placeholder rows with no real search data, and keeping them would bias every metric toward zero. No FlyRank product decision flags exist in this table, so none were used as inputs. Only hashed client_hash_id and content_hash_id identifiers appear anywhere in this analysis, no client names, domains, or query text.

ValidatedTrain (Q1) and test (Q2) are fully separate calendar quarters, no page-month appears in both.
§3

Methodology

Label

A page is marked needs_review if its impressions dropped 10% or more from the first to the last month of the quarter, and it had at least 100 total impressions across the quarter. This is the same proxy defined in Week 2, applied identically to both quarters.

Features

Average search position, click-through rate, total impressions, the within-quarter trend, and the mean CTR for that page's position bucket. Every feature is computed only from data available at scoring time.

Baseline

The Week 4 hand-written rule: three reason codes, QUICK_WIN, CTR_FIX, REFRESH, checked in order, with a volume threshold set from the train quarter's median impressions.

Model

Logistic regression, balanced class weighting (train positive rate is ~11%). Missing trend values, pages with zero impressions in their first month, were imputed with the median.

Validation design

A genuine time-aware split: train on Q1, test on Q2. Every threshold and bucket statistic was computed on train data only, then applied unchanged to test, so nothing from the held-out quarter leaked into setup.

Leakage checkTrend compares first vs. last month within the same quarter only. Bucket stats and volume threshold: train-only. Model never sees Q2 during fitting.

Read this before the table aboveAll 50 of the baseline's top-ranked pages carry the REFRESH reason code. That rule's condition (trend down 10%+, enough volume) is nearly identical to the label's own definition. The baseline isn't predicting the label here, it's largely restating it. Its 1.000 precision reflects that overlap, not genuine ranking skill, and is reported as a finding, not a result to be proud of.

A fairer test

Isolating pages the baseline does not already flag as REFRESH, the model reaches an AUC of 0.782 on a 27.1% positive rate. On the harder set, the model has real, independent signal to offer.

Independent skill, confirmed0.782 AUC on the subset where the baseline's shortcut doesn't apply. This is the number that reflects the model's actual contribution.
§5

Limitations

  • Not causal. Nothing here shows that refreshing a page causes traffic to recover, or explains how Google's ranking works. Every claim is observed and directional.
  • Baseline/label overlap. As shown in §4, the Week 4 rule's REFRESH condition mirrors the label definition closely enough to inflate its headline metrics. The subset AUC (0.782) is the more honest read of model skill.
  • Distribution shift. The test quarter's positive rate (36%) is roughly triple the train quarter's (11%). This may be a real seasonal pattern, but it means the two periods aren't identically distributed.
  • Partial data coverage. Only pages with gsc_data_available = true are represented; rows without real search data were dropped, not zero-filled.
  • Unscaled coefficients. The logistic regression was trained on raw feature scales. Coefficient magnitudes aren't directly comparable, only their signs are interpretable with confidence.
  • One-year window. The full dataset spans 2025–2026. Seasonal effects outside this window are unverified.
§6

Ranked recommendations

The model's confidence score orders all 271,046 test-quarter pages from most to least in need of review. Each page keeps its rule-based reason code as a plain-language explanation, so the queue stays explainable rather than a black-box number.

Quick win

Push toward page 1

Ranks just off page one (positions 11–20) with real volume behind it.

CTR fix

Rewrite title / meta

Ranks well, but click-through sits below what its own position bucket normally earns.

Refresh

Refresh or expand content

Losing impressions over the quarter, still enough volume to be worth the effort.

A small group of pages (3 at a model score of exactly 1.0, 1,541 above 0.99) sit at the ceiling of the model's scale, a side effect of unscaled impression counts, not a claim of certainty. These pages are very likely genuine priorities given their size, but their exact order among themselves shouldn't be read too precisely. Every flagged page is meant for a human reviewer to confirm, not an automatic trigger.
